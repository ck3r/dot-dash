1. dot-da.sh

  2. EXTENSION

Text on any page as Morse: see it, play it, copy it, or send it to the station. Morse on a page (a puzzle, a post) reads back as text. It does nothing until you ask, and keeps nothing.

get it

  • Chrome Web Store: Chrome, and browsers built on it
  • Edge Add-ons: in review
  • Firefox Add-ons: in review

use it

  • Right-click a selection, then “Morse in dot-da.sh”: a card by the selection, with each character under its code. Play it, pause it, drag along it; click the Morse to copy it. Esc stops it.
  • The toolbar button (Alt+Shift+M): the selection, or with nothing selected the page's main text, on the station's line in a new tab. While something plays, it stops it.
  • Ctrl+M (Control+M on a Mac): copy the selection as Morse.
  • The address bar: type morse, a space, then text or Morse, and Enter.

the card

  dot-da.sh / EXTENSION         share  ×
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ -- --- .-. ... .  /  -.-             │
│ M   O   R   S  E     K               │
│                     10 dits, 5 dahs  │
└─────────────────o/───────────────────┘
  ⚙  ( ▷ 00:00/00:04  ━━━━┃─────── )

The station's look, cut down to one message. The switch keys along as it plays, and it (or the name) opens the message at the station; share copies its link; the gear sets the speed and pitch. Drag the top line to move the card out of the way. For a long one (over 15 s), the button at the player's end pops it out into a small window that stays on top while you work in another (Chrome and Edge). To have it pop out by itself when you switch tabs, turn that on in the settings (the gear); it's off to start, because Chrome then asks each site's leave. Why.

what it sees

Only what you point it at, only when you do: no access to any site until you click, and nothing of it running in your pages. What it sends to the station goes after the # in the address, which a browser never sends to a server. It collects nothing. All of it, and why it asks for each permission: privacy.

nothing to install

On a phone, or a browser that won't take extensions: the bookmarklet sends a selection to the station, and that's all.
