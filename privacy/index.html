1. dot-da.sh

  2. PRIVACY

In short: what you key, type or select stays in your browser. No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no cookies, nothing sold or shared. The one exception is a link you choose to share, below.

the extension

  • It reads a page only when you ask it to: the toolbar button, its shortcut, the right-click menu, or morse in the address bar. Then it reads the selection, or the page's main text if nothing is selected. Until then it has no access to any site, and nothing of it runs in your pages.
  • Sending opens the station at dot-da.sh/#text=…. The text goes after the #, and browsers never send that part of an address to a server. The station reads it in your browser and tidies the address back to /.
  • With backlink on (its settings; on to start), the address of the page you sent from rides along the same way (from a selection, with its first and last few words, so the link opens the page at that passage) (&src=…, after the #, never to a server), and the station keeps it with the message in your browser, so the tape can link back to it. Turn it off and the text goes alone.
  • The card (playing, copying, sharing a link) works within the browser. It sends nothing anywhere.
  • One setting, off unless you turn it on, changes where the card's sound plays: "pop out by itself" (Chrome and Edge). With it on, the tone plays in the page you're reading, as ordinary audio, so the browser can pop the card out when you switch tabs; the page can tell that something is playing, but not what. The first time on a site, Chrome asks whether that site may pop out by itself: the permission is the site's, kept by Chrome, and you can take it back in the site's settings.
  • Its settings (speed, sound, the lamp, the look, backlink, the pop-out) are kept by your browser's extension storage. If you've turned on sync for extensions, your browser syncs them as it does for any other extension.
  • It collects nothing and sends nothing to us or anyone else.

why each permission

  • activeTab, scripting: to read the selection or the page you're on, once, when you click.
  • contextMenus: the right-click items.
  • clipboardWrite: copying Morse (Ctrl+M, or a click on the card) and a link (the card's share).
  • offscreen: in Chrome and Edge, a hidden page to play the tone from.
  • storage: your settings.

the site

  • Your settings, history and stats are kept in your browser (local storage) and never uploaded. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes them.
  • The profile can save your settings and stats to a file, and load one, to move them to another device. The file goes where you put it; the site never sees it. It never holds your messages.
  • Everything the site needs, fonts included, comes from dot-da.sh. Nothing is loaded from anyone else.
  • The site is hosted by GitHub Pages, with link previews made by a Cloudflare Worker. Like any web host, they may keep ordinary request logs (address, time, browser). We don't add any tracking of our own.

links you share

A share link (dot-da.sh/?/…) carries its message in the address itself, after the ?, so a chat app can show a preview and the moving picture. That part does reach the server when the link is opened or previewed. Share only what you'd put in any link. Nothing is stored there: the message is drawn from the address each time.

contact

A question about any of this, or about the site or the extension: cq@dot-da.sh.

The report in the station's settings can be sent the same way, to cq+bugs@dot-da.sh: it opens your own email app with what the report shows you first (the version, the device, the settings, the last errors; never a message), and nothing goes until you send it. Your address is used to reply, and for nothing else.
